PR Boxes and Influencer Packaging: How to Make Boxes People Film

  • By The Luxora Packaging Team
  • 3 min read
  • 28 August 2026

On this page

  2. Box styles that film well
  3. Inserts: the product should be the hero
  4. Finishes that look good on camera
  5. Quantities and timing
  6. PR box checklist

A PR box has one job: to be opened on camera. That changes how you design it. A box that looks lovely on a shelf can look flat in a video, and a box that seems simple can look amazing in a ten-second clip. Here is what we have learned from making boxes that people actually film.

Design the opening moment first

Most unboxing videos are short, so the opening has to deliver in a few seconds. Think about what the camera sees, in order: the closed box, the lid or doors moving, then the product. Each of those moments should look intentional.

Tip

Open your sample box in front of your phone camera before approving the bulk order. You will spot problems (glare, a tight lid, a hidden logo) in seconds.

Box styles that film well

  • Double-door boxes are the most dramatic. Two doors swing open and the product is standing right there.
  • Magnetic flip-top boxes give a satisfying snap and a clean, single movement.
  • Drawer boxes with a ribbon pull create suspense as the tray slides out.
  • Book-style boxes give you an inside cover for a personal note or a QR code.

Lid and base boxes can work too, but lifting a lid with one hand while filming is awkward. A shoulder-neck version helps, because the lid slides off more smoothly.

Inserts: the product should be the hero

When the box opens, every product should be visible at once and sitting exactly where you placed it. That means a fitted insert, usually EVA foam or a velvet-wrapped tray, with cut-outs for each item. Leave a small finger notch beside each product so it can be lifted out easily on camera.

Put the hero product in the centre or at the top of the layout. Supporting items go around it.

Finishes that look good on camera

Phone cameras love contrast and texture, and they struggle with shiny surfaces under ring lights.

  • Soft-touch matt wraps photograph beautifully and do not reflect lights.
  • Foil logos catch light as the box moves, which reads as luxury in video.
  • Embossing or debossing adds texture that shows up when the box is turned.
  • Printing inside the lid gives creators a second reveal and a place for your handle or hashtag.

Use full gloss sparingly. It can flare badly under lights and hide your logo.

Quantities and timing

PR runs are often small, which is exactly why our 50-piece minimum works well. Order a few more than your creator list. Some boxes will go to your own team, photographers, retailers or last-minute additions, and a reorder of five boxes is never economical. Allow time for a sample, because you will want to film it yourself before sending anything out.

PR box checklist

  1. Choose a style with a strong opening moment.
  2. Lay out the products so all are visible at once.
  3. Pick matt wraps and one light-catching finish.
  4. Add a message inside the lid.
  5. Film the sample before approving bulk.
  6. Order a few spares.

Get those right and the box does half the marketing for you.
